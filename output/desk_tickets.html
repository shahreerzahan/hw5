Reflection

This is based on the three runs I did after resetting the database: 131aba06f7e2 (101), a41472b4caf2 (102) and d24273839db7 (103). The evidence is the Actual section on each ticket tab and the Cash tab. Checking went from $3,400 to $152, and every dollar of that was a human clicking Approve on the board.

1. How the agents did on each ticket

Ticket 101: Bulldog tee for Tauhid Zaman Bmy grade

The team got the diagnosis right. Inventory saw 0 CC-TEE-WHITE in size S, found Bulldog Print Co (5 lead days), and caught that invoice 501 ($840, due 2026-08-28) was still open, which is exactly the "vendor won't ship while unpaid" rule. Accounting worked out that the invoice was 3 days overdue against desk.date_today (2026-08-31), not against the real calendar, and queued the $840 instead of paying it. Customer Service gave Tauhid a tentative 2026-09-05 (today + 5 lead days) and said plainly it wasn't promised.

What went right
  • The out-of-stock problem and the blocking invoice were both found from real MCP data.
  • Money only went into approval_queue. Nothing was paid until I approved it.
  • The customer draft didn't mention our unpaid bill or our cash; it just said "being confirmed".
  • The Boss kept the restock date tentative (2026-09-05, "not a promised delivery date" in its resolution note) instead of promising it to Tauhid.
  • The cash math is right: $3,400 − $840 − $8 = $2,552, matching what Accounting saw at the start of ticket 102.
What went wrong
  • It didn't explain its vendor choice. list_vendors returned three vendors: Bulldog Print Co (5 days), Elm City Gifts (3 days) and QuickShip CT (1 day). Inventory still went with Bulldog Print Co, the one blocked by the unpaid invoice. A faster, unblocked vendor might have avoided paying $840 just to restock one $8 tee. The vendors table lists Elm City Gifts as "mugs and small goods" and QuickShip CT as a "local courier", so Bulldog may have been the only apparel option. But Inventory never said why it ruled the other two out, so I couldn't check that decision without going to the database myself.
  • It probably ordered the same tee twice. Invoice 501's description is literally "Rush reprint CC-TEE-WHITE S", so paying it should already release the tee. Accounting still queued an extra purchase order for 1 × CC-TEE-WHITE S at $8 (request #2), and I approved it.
  • The run was probably cached. It finished in about 8 seconds and matched my pre-reset 23:42 run word for word, so Portkey most likely returned saved answers. The database changes (requests #1 and #2, draft #1) are real and came from this run, but the "reasoning" wasn't new.

Human approvals: request #1, $840 for invoice 501 (Bulldog Print Co), and request #2, an $8 purchase order. Checking went $3,400 → $2,560 → $2,552.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Ticket 102 (rent). The job is: read the ticket, read lease 1, compare 2026-09-02 to desk.date_today, check checking has $2,400, queue the payment and resolve. That's six tool calls in a straight line, with no judgement call and no customer. The team version used 3 agent runs, 17 model requests and about 30,000 input tokens (from the end entries in audit_trail.json). Facilities and Accounting each re-read the ticket, the desk date, the lease and the cash balance, so it took 12 MCP calls where about 6 would do.

One "rent agent" with get_ticket, get_desk_date, get_lease, get_cash_balance, prepare_payment and resolve_ticket would be faster, cheaper and easier to audit. There are no handoffs to lose context in, and the money guardrails live in the MCP tools anyway, not in the agent split. Human approval (request #3) would still be required.

101 and 103 are different. They cross stock, vendor bills, cash, pricing and a customer message, and on 103 the parts actually conflict: the restock needs $264 while only $152 is available, and the discount needs a margin check. Splitting that across specialists with narrow tool access is useful there. For example, Customer Service can't queue money, and only Accounting can.

4. Three new problems this team could solve with my tools

Another out-of-stock order
A student orders a Crest Mug (CC-MUG-CREST, 0 in stock). This is the 101 path: check_stock → list_vendors finds Elm City Gifts ("mugs and small goods", 3 lead days) → get_vendor_invoices → Accounting runs prepare_purchase_order at the $3.50 unit cost → Customer Service drafts a tentative date of today + 3 days.

With only $152 in checking, a small mug order still fits under the cash guardrail.

A new vendor invoice coming due
Bulldog sends a new bill. Accounting uses get_invoice, compares the due date to desk.date_today, checks get_cash_balance (including pending items) and queues it with prepare_payment. If it's more than $152, the tool refuses and the Boss has to decide what waits, the same trade-off as the 103 refusal.
A bulk discount request
Say another club wants 10 CC-HAT-BLUE caps. There are 40 in stock, so no restock is needed. Accounting checks the margin on the $6 cost and $22 list price using check_stock, Customer Service drafts the counteroffer, and the Boss resolves it. Exactly the 103 tools, without the cash problem.

5. Three new problems it could not solve, and what I'd need

Tracking sales and incoming revenue
We only model money going out. That's why checking only ever fell ($3,400 → $152), even though selling those 8 hoodies on 103 would have brought money in. There's no sales or orders table and no tool to record a sale.

Need: a sales table, record_sale and get_revenue MCP tools, and a Revenue agent so the cash forecast includes money coming in.

Actually sending emails or placing vendor orders
Drafts #1 and #2 are still sitting in message_drafts, and the approved $8 tee order was never sent to Bulldog. "No real emails or vendor calls" is on purpose, so the team can't finish the loop.

Need: send_email and place_vendor_order tools that only run on drafts and orders a human has approved, the same way the approve route gates cash today, with every send logged in the audit trail.

Planning stock ahead of game days and graduation
Ticket 103 was reactive: we found out we were 12 hoodies short when the club asked. Nothing looks ahead to Harvard–Yale or Commencement weekends, when demand spikes.

Need: sales history, a campus events calendar, a forecast_demand tool, and a Planning agent that suggests purchase orders early, while there's still cash and Bulldog's 5-day lead time can be met.

6. How I'd fix what went wrong

  • Avoid duplicate orders (101). Make prepare_purchase_order check open invoices for the same SKU and size and refuse, or at least warn, when one already covers it. Invoice 501's "Rush reprint CC-TEE-WHITE S" should have blocked the extra $8 order. Add a line to Accounting's prompt: "if an open invoice is already for this item, paying it is the restock".
  • Make vendor choices visible (101). Inventory's report should list every vendor it considered and why each was or wasn't usable (specialty, lead time, open invoices). Then I can see whether a faster, unblocked vendor could have restocked the tee without first clearing the $840 invoice.
  • Don't promise dates that aren't backed (103). Customer Service should call list_pending_approvals (or a new restock-status tool) before giving a date. If the restock was refused or isn't approved, the draft should say "we'll confirm a date once the restock is ordered", not "expected by September 5".
  • Give pricing a range instead of the floor (103). Accounting should return a range: open near list price, with a small bulk discount (for example around $50 a hoodie, about 14% off), and keep $36.67 as the walk-away floor. The floor goes to the Boss and never into a customer draft.
  • Turn off caching for real runs (101). Send Portkey's no-cache/force-refresh option on real ticket runs, so every run actually reasons from the current database. Caching could stay on for demos and testing.
  • Watch the cash cushion. After 102, only $152 was left. Accounting should flag when a payment would leave less than a set minimum (say $500), so I know before approving that it will block the next restock.
  • Cut repeat lookups. Let the Boss pass the facts it already has (ticket, desk date) to specialists, and tell specialists not to re-read them. That would have saved about half the MCP calls on 102.
